How tau_i is determined (II.4.1, C.17; `rafr.validity.tau.fit_tau`):

* fitted on D_fit against y*; the judgment is `y = 1[s >= tau_i]`;
* candidate thresholds: the distinct D_fit scores in ascending order, the midpoint of
  every adjacent pair of them, plus the two ends -- the smallest distinct score (every
  state flagged) and the largest plus half the last gap (no state flagged); a single
  distinct score is the only candidate;
* criterion: F1 over the candidates. The candidates whose F1 lies within 1e-12 of the
  maximum, taken in ascending order, form runs of adjacent candidates; each maximal run
  is one plateau. tau_i is the midpoint of the widest plateau (width = its threshold
  span; on a tie, the lowest); a one-candidate plateau gives that candidate itself;
* F2, PR-AUC, precision, recall and balanced accuracy are reported alongside and take
  no part in the choice.

Not applied here: `score()` returns the raw score.

## Determinism

Same `(model_version, prompt hash, text)` gives the same score bit for bit:

* `model.eval()` and `torch.no_grad()`; nothing is sampled on the scoring path
  (greedy decoding happens only in the preflight that settles the verdict tokens);
* attention implementation `eager`, recorded per model;
* left padding with position ids rebuilt from the mask; every batch padded to a fixed number of rows and to one fixed length per call type, so a score cannot depend on its neighbours;
* TF32 off, cuDNN deterministic and not benchmarking,
  `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True, warn_only=True)`, `CUBLAS_WORKSPACE_CONFIG`;
* on every load the two invented direction-check sentences are re-scored and must equal
  the probe's recorded scores exactly, or the factory refuses to score.
